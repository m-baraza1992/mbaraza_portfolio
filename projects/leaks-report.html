Leaks & Bursts Incident Report

Every leak or burst pipe reported in the field becomes a record in MOWASSCO's GIS — but a record is only useful if it's accurate and complete. This report set out to answer a simple question with real operational weight: how well is that record-keeping actually holding up across the utility's schemes?

The problem

Field teams are focused, understandably, on fixing the leak in front of them — not on administrative follow-up. That meant repairs were often completed on the ground without the corresponding GIS record ever being closed out, leaving the system showing incidents as still "open" long after they'd actually been resolved.

The approach

I compiled and analyzed 249 leak and burst incidents recorded between January and June 2026, across seven of MOWASSCO's schemes. The report went through several structural revisions based on supervisor feedback before being approved for distribution — refining it from a raw incident log into something management could actually act on.

A key finding was the gap between field completion and system closure: repairs were routinely finished without the matching GIS record being updated, meaning the data was systematically overstating how many leaks were still active at any given time.

Tools

ArcGIS Enterprise · spatial analysis of incident records · structured reporting for management review

Outcome

The report was formally submitted and approved, with its record-closure finding informing a recommendation to tighten field-to-GIS handoff procedures. I also assessed converting the findings into an ArcGIS StoryMap for presentation purposes, alongside the formal Word/PDF report used for official record-keeping.
